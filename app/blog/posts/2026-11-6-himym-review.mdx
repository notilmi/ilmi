## SPOILER ALERT 🚨

Lately, I've been watching a lot—and I mean *a lot*—of TV shows: *Silicon Valley* (2014–2019), *The Big Bang Theory* (2006–2019), *Modern Family* (2009–2020), and more. But to be completely honest, there's one show that I genuinely fell in love with: as the title gives away, **How I Met Your Mother**.

The show follows an unforgettable group of friends in New York City: Ted Mosby, Marshall Eriksen, Barney Stinson, Lily Aldrin, and Robin Scherbatsky. What makes this series so memorable is how distinct and quirky each of them is:
- **Ted Mosby**: The architect and hopeless romantic
- **Marshall Eriksen**: The genuinely good, pure-hearted guy (seriously!)
- **Barney Stinson**: The serial womanizer who unexpectedly became the most compelling character—even though the writers did him dirty in the finale
- **Lily Aldrin**: The depiction of a woman who grappled with sacrificing her artistic ambitions for family life
- **Robin Scherbatsky**: A fierce Canadian determined to climb the ranks and become a respected journalist in NYC

Throughout its 9-season run, the show is an absolute joy. It’s packed with iconic running gags that stay with you forever, along with genuine character growth that proves this is *not* just another generic sitcom.

But the most fascinating part of the show (**SPOILER ALERT!**) is the emotional depth the writers baked into the narrative. On the surface—to the naked eye—it looks like standard sitcom comfort food: a close-knit group having fun and making us laugh. But scratch beneath the surface, and each character carries a profound, lingering grief that gives them rare emotional depth:

![Griefs](/blog/2026-11-06-himym-review/robin.png)

- **Ted Mosby**: Despite being rejected by Robin in the literal pilot, he spends years doing everything he can to win her heart. They date for a season, break up, and ostensibly move on—yet deep down, despite all his efforts, he simply cannot let Robin go.
- **Marshall Eriksen**: The sweetest, most optimistic guy, who completely breaks down when sudden tragic news hits: his father, best friend, and role model, Marvin Eriksen Sr., has passed away. Marshall is devastated by the thought that his dad left no meaningful last words. And even after discovering the accidental pocket-dial voicemail where Marvin's last words were *"I love you,"* Marshall chooses to keep that intimate moment to himself throughout the funeral.
- **Barney Stinson**: A serial womanizer who is quietly trapped in denial. His hyper-fixation on suits, elaborate playbook tricks, meaningless one-night stands, and pathological urge to tell exaggerated "awesome" and "legendary" stories are just shields to keep his inner pain at bay—driving him to constantly retreat into denial.
- **Lily Aldrin**: A woman who sacrificed so much of her life and career to become a mother. Despite loving her family, there are heartbreaking moments where she confesses that she sometimes wishes she weren't a mother, secretly longing for that alternate path where she took the gamble in San Francisco to become the artist she always dreamed of being.
- **Robin Scherbatsky**: A former Canadian pop star who moved to New York to build a serious journalism career. Despite her career progression, Robin quietly mourns heavy losses: going through a painful breakup with Barney only to watch him flaunt his womanizing lifestyle (leaving her feeling like just another "number"), all while silently suffering the painful reality that she cannot have children.

What makes this series truly unforgettable to me is the subversion of expectations. We all tuned in expecting a straightforward story about how Ted Mosby met the mother of his children. Instead, what we actually received was a bittersweet chronicle of five friends sharing their youth, mistakes, and joys together.

And to top it all off, if you connect the dots behind their underlying struggles, each character mirrors a stage of the Kübler-Ross model (also known as the five stages of grief):
- **Barney Stinson** - Denial
- **Robin Scherbatsky** - Anger
- **Ted Mosby** - Bargaining
- **Marshall Eriksen** - Depression
- **Lily Aldrin** - Acceptance

Of course, across 9 seasons, we can all agree there were plenty of controversial plotlines—especially the polarizing finale and certain character directions. But I’ll save those details for part two of this review!
